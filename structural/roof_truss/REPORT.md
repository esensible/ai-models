# 8.5 m coupled-rafter roof, 27°, 35×105 Oregon at 1200 c/c — findings

Generated by `roof_truss.py --all --plots` (full tables: `report/analysis.md`).
Indicative only — see README for assumptions and how to change them.

## Short answer

**35×105 Oregon does not work for this roof at 8.5 m span and 1200 c/c in any
support condition modelled.** The frame as described has three independent
problems, in order of severity:

1. **Horizontal thrust.** A collar tie 1 m below the apex cannot tie the feet of
   8.5 m rafters. If the walls can't resist thrust (timber stud walls can't), the
   frame spreads: the model predicts ~540 mm of long-term wall spread and rafter
   moments 67× capacity (`roller` case). If the walls *do* resist it, each truss
   pushes **5.7 kN** horizontally into the top of each wall under design loads
   (1.1 kN per truss under dead load alone).
2. **Rafter bending.** Even with the thrust fully resisted (`pinned`), asymmetric
   wind (pressure one slope, suction the other) bends the rafters to
   **M\* ≈ 2.2 kNm against a capacity of 0.62 kNm → 12.7× over**. A three-pin
   arch resists unsymmetric load by bending; the collar tie only carries
   horizontal force and can't help. The rafter would need to be about
   **45×190 F7** (utilisation 0.91) to survive this case unassisted.
3. **Collar tie buckling.** With the thrust resisted, the collar acts as a
   *strut*, not a tie: 5.8 kN compression on a 3.9 m long 35 mm thick member →
   k12 ≈ 0.02, utilisation **8.5×**. Two intermediate lateral restraints (e.g.
   ceiling battens / a runner at third points) bring it to 1.00. It only becomes a
   tension tie once the walls are free to spread, which is the case that fails
   catastrophically.

The deflection numbers, by contrast, are fine wherever the strength checks
pass: rafter dead-load deflection 4 mm long-term against a 16 mm limit.

## What does work

The conventional coupled-roof arrangement, which is what AS 1684 assumes:

- **Ceiling joists / bottom ties at rafter-foot level**, connected to each rafter
  foot for ~3.4 kN tension per truss (that's only 12 % of a 35×105's tension
  capacity; the connection governs — nail/bolt it as a tension splice). The
  ties must be supported along their length (internal wall, hanging beam) — an
  unsupported 35×105 over 8.5 m sags 150 mm under its own weight.
- **Strut the under-purlins** down to an internal wall or strutting beam at
  every truss (struts at every second truss would put 1.8 kNm into a 35×105
  purlin spanning 2.4 m, capacity 0.79 kNm). The purlin then acts as the
  intermediate rafter support the name implies and the frame no longer relies
  on arch action: wall thrust drops to 1.9 kN per truss and the asymmetric-wind
  moment disappears. Strut reaction ≈ 3.0 kN per truss, uplift −2.8 kN (tie the
  struts down).
- **With struts and ties, the rafters at 1200 c/c need to be 35×140 F7**
  (utilisation 0.79, governed by the 1.4 kN concentrated roof live load on the
  2.57 m lower segment). 45×140 F7 gives 0.59. 35×105 is 1.76 — it fails.
- **To keep 35×105 rafters**, shorten the rafter span: a 35×105 F7 rafter at
  1200 c/c is good for about **1.5 m** between supports under the 1.4 kN load
  (P·L/4 ≤ 0.79 kNm; ~1.6 m with continuity over the supports). That means two purlin/strut
  lines per rafter (≈ 1.6 m up the slope and ≈ 3.2 m), or rafters at 600 c/c
  instead (halves the UDL cases but not the point load — the point load still
  governs, so the span limit barely moves). Deeper rafters are the cheaper fix.
- The collar tie at 1 m below the apex is then a lightly loaded member
  (≤ 1 kN, utilisation 0.23) and 35×105 is fine for it; 3 nails per end.

## Sensitivities worth knowing

- **Wind class.** Defaults are indicative N2 pressures. N3 multiplies all wind
  results by ~1.56; N1 by ~0.72. The asymmetric case (+0.5 / −0.8 kPa) assumes
  internal suction with a windward pressure — appropriate for a building with
  openings; a sealed building is closer to +0.1 / −0.8, which cuts the rafter
  moment by ~15 %. None of this changes the conclusions.
- **Grade.** F8 (select Oregon) raises capacities ~20 %; F5 lowers them ~22 %.
  No change in conclusions.
- **φ.** 0.70 is the visually-graded F-grade, category-2 value. Using 0.85
  (in-grade-tested products, e.g. MGP) scales utilisations by 0.82.
- Birdsmouth notches at the heel, bearing on the plate, and the
  rafter-to-purlin and strut connections are not checked and all need a look in
  the real design.
