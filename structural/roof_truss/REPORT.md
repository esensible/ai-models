# 8.5 m coupled-rafter roof, 27°, 35×105 Oregon at 1200 c/c — findings

Structure as described (suburban Adelaide, wind class N1): rafters at 1200 c/c
on wall plates 8.5 m apart; ceiling joists at the rafter feet tying the walls,
carrying a plasterboard ceiling, supported by a mid wall and by deep hanging
beams; a collar tie 1 m below the apex; 35×105 under-purlins under the rafters
just below the collar junction and a 40×190 ridge beam, both bearing on the
room cross walls, which sit mid-way between trusses at 3.6 m centres (three
trusses per bay). Corrugated iron roof; everything else 35×105
Oregon (F7 assumed).

The model is a grillage: three 3.6 m bays of trusses (nine trusses) coupled
vertically to the two under-purlins and the ridge beam, which are continuous
beams on the cross walls. Results are for the truss in the middle of the middle
bay. Generated by `roof_truss.py --all --plots`; full tables and plots in
`report/`. Indicative only — see README for assumptions.

## Short answer

The cross walls and the ridge beam are doing real work: the rafters go from
2.7× over to 1.7×, and the ridge beam itself passes. But **the 35×105
under-purlins are too flexible to prop the trusses across a 3.6 m bay**, so they
neither save the rafters nor the collar, and they overload themselves trying.

| Member | Governing case | Times over capacity | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Collar tie** | dead + roof live load | **8.6×** | still squeezed into compression (3.7 kN) by the rafters; 3.9 m of unbraced 35 mm timber buckles sideways |
| **Under-purlins** | dead + asymmetric wind | **3.7×** | 1.9 kNm hogging over the cross walls vs 0.51 kNm capacity; a 35×105 can't act as a 3.6 m beam carrying three trusses |
| **Rafters** | dead + 1.4 kN point load | **1.7×** | 2.57 m lower segment, partially propped by the soft purlin |
| Ceiling joists (mid wall + hanging beams) | dead + 0.9 kN | 1.08 | borderline; passes with the conservative top-edge restraint assumption relaxed, or beams a little closer than quarter points |
| Ridge beam 40×190 | dead + roof live | 0.95 | passes, just; carries 3.9 kN to each interior cross wall |

Deflections pass everywhere (rafter 4.6 mm long-term against 16 mm; purlin
2.2 mm against 12 mm; joists 2.3 mm against 7 mm). It is a strength problem.

## What the cross walls change

With the under-purlins and ridge continuous over walls every 3.6 m, the
trusses near the walls are well propped and the ones mid-bay much less so; load
flows to whichever path is stiffer. The 40×190 ridge is stiff enough to take a
useful share (3.9 kN per interior wall under design load), which is why the
rafter result improves. The 35×105 purlins deflect with the trusses (2.2 mm
long-term at mid-bay) instead of holding them, so the frame still works mostly
as an arch, the collar still ends up in compression, and the purlin picks up
bending it can't carry. Compare the what-ifs:

| Configuration (35×105 F7) | Rafter | Collar | Purlin | Joist | Ridge |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **As built** (purlins + ridge on 3.6 m cross walls, joists on mid wall + beams) | **1.75** | **8.6** | **3.7** | 1.08 | 0.95 |
| Joists on the mid wall only (no hanging beams) | 1.75 | 8.6 | 3.7 | **3.8** | 0.95 |
| No ridge beam | 1.8 | 9.1 | 3.8 | 1.09 | – |
| Purlins as bracing only (no cross walls) | **2.7** | 8.0 | – | 1.08 | – |
| Every collar node rigidly propped (ideal stiff purlins) | 1.35 | 0.23 | – | 1.04 | – |
| As built + collar braced at third points | 1.75 | **1.02** | 3.7 | 1.08 | 0.95 |

The "rigidly propped" row is the target: if the purlins were stiff enough to
hold the collar nodes, the collar's compression vanishes (0.23) and the rafters
come down to 1.35×. The gap between that row and the as-built row is entirely
the purlin's flexibility.

## What would make it work

Upsizing one member at a time (everything else as built, 35×105 F7):

| Change | Rafter | Collar | Purlin | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| none (as built) | 1.75 | 8.6 | 3.7 | |
| rafters 35×140 only | 1.04 | 8.6 | 3.1 | stiffer rafters attract load; purlin eases a little, collar unchanged |
| rafters 45×190 only | 0.59 | 8.5 | 2.1 | same story, more so |
| purlins 45×190 only | 1.57 | 7.0 | 1.18 | purlins pass; they are still not stiff enough to relieve the collar |
| purlins 45×240 + rafters 35×140 | 0.87 | 5.5 | 0.96 | even a 45×240 purlin over 3.6 m is far from a rigid prop |
| collar braced at third points only | 1.75 | 1.02 | 3.7 | collar fixed, nothing else |

So stronger rafters are the smallest part of the fix, and no practical purlin
makes the collar's compression go away. The collar needs its own fix. A
complete package that passes everything:

- **Under-purlins 45×190 F7** (1.15 with the stiffer rafters; 45×240 gives
  0.96) — or keep 35×105 and strut each purlin down to the hanging beam at
  every truss, then check the hanging beams for ~3 kN per strut.
- **Rafters 35×140 F7** (0.90). 35×105 stays over because of the 1.4 kN
  maintenance point load on the 2.57 m lower segment.
- **Collar tie laterally braced at third points** (0.86) — a runner along the
  building nailed to the collars, since ceiling battens are 1.2 m below. A
  doubled collar (two 35×105 bolted together) with one mid-length restraint
  also works (0.48).
- Joists as they are, with the hanging beams at or inside quarter span (1.07).
- Ridge beam 40×190 as it is (0.61 once the purlins carry their share).
- Keep the rafter-foot-to-joist connection as a nailed or bolted tension
  splice for 2.4 kN per truss.

## Laminating instead of replacing

Doubling members by nailing a second 35×105 face to face (70×105) is attractive
because it can be done in place. Checked combinations:

| Laminated | Rafter | Collar | Purlin | Ridge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rafters + collars doubled, collar runner at mid-length | 0.92 | 0.56 | **3.25** | 0.93 |
| … + purlins doubled (70×105) | 0.88 | 0.57 | 1.20 | 0.85 |
| … + purlins tripled (105×105) | 0.86 | 0.57 | 0.86 | 0.79 |
| … + purlins replaced with 45×190 | 0.81 | 0.50 | 1.18 | 0.62 |
| … + purlins replaced with 35×190 | 0.83 | 0.52 | 2.38 | 0.66 |

Doubling the rafters and collars solves those two members. The purlins need
either three plies, or a doubled purlin plus one strut per bay down to the
hanging beam, or a 45×240 replacement. Lamination fixings: nail the plies
together at ≤ 600 mm staggered so they buckle as one; the plies share bending
without needing composite action.

## Sensitivities

- **Wind.** N1 as confirmed. Wind now only governs the purlins (asymmetric
  case); the collar and rafters are gravity-governed, so wind class barely
  matters.
- **Grade.** F8 raises capacities ~20 %, F5 lowers ~22 %. Conclusions unchanged.
- **φ.** 0.70 (visually graded F-grade, category 2). 0.85 scales results by 0.82.
- **Cross-wall spacing.** Walls mid-way between trusses → 3.6 m bays, 600 mm
  from wall to nearest truss. Purlin bending scales roughly with the square of
  the bay (4.0 m gave 4.2×).
- Birdsmouth notches, bearing, rafter-to-joist, purlin-to-wall and ridge
  bearing connections are not checked.
