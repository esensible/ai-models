# 8.5 m coupled-rafter roof, 27°, 35×105 Oregon at 1200 c/c — findings

Structure as described: rafters at 1200 c/c on wall plates 8.5 m apart, ceiling
joists at the rafter feet tying the walls and carrying a ceiling, a collar tie
1 m below the apex, and transverse under-purlins at the collar node tying the
trusses together (bracing, not supports). Corrugated iron roof, everything
35×105 Oregon (F7 assumed).

Generated by `roof_truss.py --all --plots` (full tables: `report/analysis.md`).
Indicative only — see README for assumptions and how to change them.

## Short answer

**35×105 is not adequate for this roof at 8.5 m span and 1200 c/c.** Three
members fail independently, each by a wide margin. The ceiling joists tying the
feet are the right idea and fix the thrust problem (the frame would otherwise
spread ~540 mm), but they do not fix the rafters or the collar.

| Member | Governing case | Demand / capacity | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ceiling joist** (unsupported 8.5 m) | 1.2G + 0.9 kN ceiling point load | **627×**, sags 1.25 m | 35×105 can't span 8.5 m; even propped mid-span it is **14×** over and sags 31 mm |
| **Rafter** | 1.2G + asymmetric wind | **12.7×** (M\* 2.2 kNm vs 0.62) | wind pressure on one slope and suction on the other bends the whole frame; the collar can't help |
| **Collar tie** | 1.2G + wind down | **8.5×** | it is a *strut* under gravity: 5.8 kN compression on 3.9 m of 35 mm timber buckles sideways |

Deflections are fine wherever strength passes (rafter 4.6 mm long-term against a
16 mm limit), so this is a strength problem, not a stiffness one.

## Why each member fails

**Ceiling joists.** With the joists tying the feet the wall thrust (3.4 kN per
truss) goes into the joists as tension, which they carry easily (12 % of
capacity; the rafter-foot connection is the hard part — nail or bolt it as a
3.4 kN tension splice). But a 35×105 joist spanning 8.5 m under its own weight
plus a plasterboard ceiling is hopeless. Propped mid-span (internal wall or
hanging beam) it still fails: 1.39 kNm demand vs 0.37 kNm capacity, with the
compression edge unrestrained over 4.25 m. Propped at third points (2.83 m spans)
it is 2.9×; 35×105 ceiling joists at 1200 c/c need supports at roughly 1.5 m.

**Rafters.** With the feet tied, the two rafters form a three-pin arch. Symmetric
loads are carried mostly by arch action and the rafter moments are small
(~0.1 kNm dead load). Asymmetric wind — pressure on the windward slope, suction
on the leeward — can only be resisted by bending the rafters, and the collar tie
carries only horizontal force so contributes nothing. The 2.2 kNm demand was
checked by hand statics. A 45×190 F7 rafter (utilisation 0.90) is what survives
this case; 35×140 does not (5.6×).

**Collar tie.** Once the walls are tied, the collar is squeezed between the two
rafters as they bow inward: 5.8 kN compression under gravity plus wind-down. It
only becomes a tension tie when the walls spread, which is the case the joists
prevent. Unbraced over 3.9 m in its weak direction k12 ≈ 0.02 and it buckles.
Two intermediate lateral restraints (ceiling battens / a runner at third points)
bring it to 1.00. The under-purlins tie the trusses together at the collar node
but don't restrain the collar's mid-length. Under asymmetric wind it sees 6.6 kN
tension — the lap needs ~20 nails each end or bolts.

## What would make it work

Two realistic routes:

**A. Keep the geometry, resize members** (walls tied by joists, purlins as
bracing): rafters ≥ **45×190 F7**, collar restrained at third points (or
45×190 as well), joists sized for their actual propped span (e.g. 45×190 F7 for
a mid-span prop, utilisation 1.0; 45×240 for 0.8). This is a lot of timber.

**B. Strut the under-purlins down to a load-bearing wall or strutting beam**
(what AS 1684 assumes an under-purlin does). This turns the rafters into
continuous beams over the purlin, kills the arch action and the asymmetric-wind
moment, and takes the collar down to 1 kN (utilisation 0.23, 3 nails each end).
Then:
- rafters at 1200 c/c need **35×140 F7** (0.79, governed by the 1.4 kN
  concentrated roof live load on the 2.57 m lower segment); 35×105 is 1.76.
  Alternatively keep 35×105 with a second purlin line so no rafter span exceeds
  ~1.5 m.
- struts at every truss (3.0 kN per strut, 2.6 kN uplift — tie them down); a
  35×105 purlin can't carry a strut reaction across 2.4 m.
- joists still need their own supports as above.

Either way, the thrust still goes into the joists (3.4 kN per truss) — keep the
foot connection.

## Sensitivities worth knowing

- **Wind class.** Defaults are indicative N2 pressures. N3 multiplies wind
  results by ~1.56; N1 by ~0.72. The asymmetric case (+0.5 / −0.8 kPa) assumes
  internal suction with windward pressure (a building with openings); a sealed
  building is nearer +0.1 / −0.8, cutting the rafter moment ~15 %. No change in
  conclusions.
- **Grade.** F8 (select Oregon) raises capacities ~20 %; F5 lowers them ~22 %.
- **φ.** 0.70 is the visually-graded F-grade, category-2 value; 0.85 (in-grade
  tested products such as MGP) scales utilisations by 0.82.
- **Ceiling.** 0.12 kPa plasterboard + 0.9 kN concentrated. A heavier lining or
  stored loads in the roof space make the joists worse.
- Birdsmouth notches, bearing on the plate, rafter-to-joist, purlin and strut
  connections are not checked and all need a look in the real design.
